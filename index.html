This study closes the structural design loop for a 3D diamond mountain-bicycle frame manufactured from 6061-T6 aluminum alloy (\( \rho = 2700\,\mathrm{kg/m}^3 \), \( E = 68.3\,\mathrm{GPa} \), \( S_y = 276\,\mathrm{MPa} \)). 2. Engineering Motivation & Optimization Problem Formulation

A bicycle frame must transfer high cyclic pedaling torque at the bottom bracket and impulsive rider weight at the saddle and head tube to the front and rear dropouts while minimizing structural mass. Manual tube selection leaves lightly loaded rear stays over-designed while risking premature buckling or excessive bottom-bracket flex under out-of-saddle sprinting loads.

2.1 Mathematical Statement (Nested Analysis and Design — NAND)

We formulate the structural design problem in reduced-space (NAND) form where the state \( \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^{108} \) is implicitly determined by the discretized 3D elasticity equilibrium equations:

$$ \begin{aligned} \min_{\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^{25}} \quad & f(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{m=1}^{10} \rho_{\mathrm{Al}} \, A_m(\mathbf{d}) \, L_m(\mathbf{d}) \\ \text{subject to} \quad & \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{d})\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{d}) = \mathbf{0} \\ & g_i(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{d})) \le 0, \quad i = 1, \dots, 11 \\ & \mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{lower}} \le \mathbf{d} \le \mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{upper}} \end{aligned} $$

Each circular hollow section (CHS) member has outer diameter \( D_g \), wall thickness \( t_g \), inner diameter \( D_{i,g} = D_g - 2t_g \), cross-sectional area \( A_g = \frac{\pi}{4}(D_g^2 - D_{i,g}^2) \), second moment of area \( I_g = \frac{\pi}{64}(D_g^4 - D_{i,g}^4) \), polar moment \( J_g = 2I_g \), and radius of gyration \( r_{g,g} = \sqrt{I_g/A_g} \).

3. Finite Element Analysis Model & Governing Physics

The structural solver is written from scratch in Python (frame_element.py, assembly.py, analysis.py) using double-precision scipy.sparse CSR matrices.

  • Governing PDE & Element Formulation: 3D linear elasticity reduced to 12-DOF two-node Euler-Bernoulli spatial frame elements (\( u_x, u_y, u_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z \) per node) capturing axial extension, Saint-Venant torsion, and biaxial cubic Hermite bending. Local member axes select the least-aligned global basis vector to eliminate singularities on vertical/near-vertical members.
  • Discretization & Topology: 8 primary structural joints (\( N_1\dots N_8 \)) connected by 10 physical members (\( M_1 \): seat tube, \( M_2 \): top tube, \( M_3 \): down tube, \( M_4 \): head tube, \( M_5\text{--}M_6 \): chainstays, \( M_7\text{--}M_8 \): seatstays, \( M_9\text{--}M_{10} \): fork blades). Each member is subdivided into 2 beam elements (20 elements, 18 nodes, 108 global DOFs) so interior bending and member-level buckling modes are resolved.
  • Boundary Conditions (Statically Determinate Dropout Supports): Rear left dropout \( N_1 \) pinned (\( u_x = u_y = u_z = 0 \)); rear right dropout \( N_2 \) constrained vertically (\( u_z = 0 \)); front left dropout \( N_7 \) constrained laterally and vertically (\( u_y = u_z = 0 \)); front right dropout \( N_8 \) constrained vertically (\( u_z = 0 \)). This prevents rigid-body drift without inducing artificial thermal/axially-clamped reaction artifacts across the dropout axles.
  • Multi-Load-Case Envelope:
    1. LC1 (Seated Ride): \( F_z = -1544.6\,\mathrm{N} \) at seat cluster \( N_4 \), \( F_z = -662.0\,\mathrm{N} \) at head-tube top \( N_5 \), plus distributed self-weight (\( g = 9.80665\,\mathrm{m/s}^2 \)).
    2. LC2_POS (Out-of-Saddle Sprint, Right Pedal): \( F_z = -1800.0\,\mathrm{N} \) and pedaling torque \( M_y = +250.0\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m} \) at bottom bracket \( N_3 \), lateral handlebar pull \( F_y = +300.0\,\mathrm{N} \) at \( N_5 \), plus self-weight.
    3. LC2_NEG (Out-of-Saddle Sprint, Left Pedal): Mirrored asymmetric loading (\( F_z = -1800.0\,\mathrm{N}, M_y = -250.0\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m} \) at \( N_3 \); \( F_y = -300.0\,\mathrm{N} \) at \( N_5 \)).
  • Key Modeling Assumptions: Rigid welded joints at frame intersections; isotropic homogeneous 6061-T6 aluminum (\( E = 68.3\,\mathrm{GPa}, \nu = 0.33, \rho = 2700\,\mathrm{kg/m}^3 \)); uniform prismatic cross-sections per tube group; transverse shear deformation neglected (\( L/D > 10 \)).

5. Adjoint Sensitivity Derivations, Gradient Verification & Computational Cost

5.1 Discrete Adjoint Formulation

Consider any scalar performance functional \( J(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{d})) \) (such as nodal displacement \( u_{z,N4} \) or the Kreisselmeier-Steinhauser stress aggregate \( \mathrm{KS}_\rho \)) governed by the linear state equation \( \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{d})\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{d}) = \mathbf{0} \). Augmenting \( J \) with the adjoint vector \( \boldsymbol{\lambda} \) gives \( \mathcal{L} = J(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{u}) + \boldsymbol{\lambda}^T(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{F}) \). Enforcing \( \partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} \) yields the symmetric adjoint linear system on the free degrees of freedom:

$$ \mathbf{K}_{ff}^T \boldsymbol{\lambda}_f = -\left( \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mathbf{u}_f} \right)^T, \qquad \frac{dJ}{dd_j} = \frac{\partial J}{\partial d_j} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}_f^T \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}_f}{\partial d_j} - \frac{\partial \mathbf{K}_{ff}}{\partial d_j}\mathbf{u}_f \right) $$

For eigenproblem constraints, implicit differentiation avoids differentiating through the iterative Lanczos solver (eigsh):

  • Natural Frequency (\( C_5 \)): For \( \mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{\phi}_1 = \omega_1^2 \mathbf{M}\boldsymbol{\phi}_1 \) normalized with \( \boldsymbol{\phi}_1^T\mathbf{M}\boldsymbol{\phi}_1 = 1 \), $$\frac{d(\omega_1^2)}{dd_j} = \boldsymbol{\phi}_1^T \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{K}}{\partial d_j} - \omega_1^2 \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}}{\partial d_j} \right) \boldsymbol{\phi}_1$$
  • Linear Buckling (\( C_6 \), Nonlinear \( N_1 \)): Writing \( (-\mathbf{K}_G(\mathbf{u}))\boldsymbol{\psi} = \mu \mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{\psi} \) with \( \mu = 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \) and \( \boldsymbol{\psi}^T\mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{\psi} = 1 \), $$\frac{d\mu}{dd_j} = \boldsymbol{\psi}^T \left( -\frac{\partial \mathbf{K}_G}{\partial d_j} - \mu \frac{\partial \mathbf{K}}{\partial d_j} \right) \boldsymbol{\psi} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{buck}}^T \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial d_j} - \frac{\partial \mathbf{K}}{\partial d_j}\mathbf{u} \right)$$ where the additional adjoint vector \( \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{buck}} \) accounts for the implicit dependence of member axial forces \( N_e(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{d})) \) in \( \mathbf{K}_G \) on the static displacement state \( \mathbf{u} \).

6. Optimization Setup, Convergence History & KKT Interpretation

In optimization.py, design variables are normalized to unity at the starting design (\( x_j = d_j / d_{j,0} \)) so all decision variables and box bounds reside on \( O(1) \) scales. Inequalities are formulated in standard smooth form \( g_i(\mathbf{x}) \le 0 \) and solved via SciPy's SLSQP (ftol = 1e-8).

6.1 Active Constraints & KKT Multiplier Interpretation

At convergence (Iteration 45, \( m^* = 1.05974\,\mathrm{kg} \)), the KKT complementarity conditions \( \mu_i^* g_i(\mathbf{x}^*) = 0 \) identify three active physical/manufacturing boundaries (\( \mu_i^* > 0 \), \( |g_i| < 10^{-8} \)) and several bound-active tube variables:

  • Active Constraint 1 — Aggregated von Mises Stress (stress_KS, \( g_1 = +7.00\times 10^{-9} \approx 0 \)): Driven by bottom-bracket torsion and asymmetric sprint bending in LC2_POS / LC2_NEG. Note that because \( \mathrm{KS}_{\rho=100} \) is a conservative smooth upper bound, the true physical maximum stress utilization is \( 0.99942 \le 1.0 \) (strictly feasible physically, \( \sigma_{vM,\max} = 137.92\,\mathrm{MPa} < 138.0\,\mathrm{MPa} \)).
  • Active Constraint 2 — Bottom Bracket Resultant Stiffness (bottom_bracket_resultant_displacement, \( g_4 = +2.99\times 10^{-10} \approx 0 \)): Reaches the exact \( \delta_{BB} = 2.000\,\mathrm{mm} \) serviceability limit, which forces the optimizer to increase chainstay outer diameter from \( 22.0\,\mathrm{mm} \) to \( 34.47\,\mathrm{mm} \) (+56.7%) and fork blade diameter from \( 32.0\,\mathrm{mm} \) to \( 41.77\,\mathrm{mm} \) (+30.5%) while thinning their walls to \( 1.00\text{--}1.04\,\mathrm{mm} \) to maximize bending inertia \( I \propto D^3 t \) per unit mass.
  • Active Constraint 3 — Thin-Wall Manufacturing Ratio (maximum_D_over_t, \( g_7 = +1.90\times 10^{-10} \approx 0 \)): Down tube (\( 46.57 / 1.164 = 40.00 \)), head tube (\( 41.13 / 1.031 = 39.90 \)), and fork (\( 41.77 / 1.044 = 40.00 \)) hit the upper \( D/t = 40.0 \) local buckling/hydroforming ceiling. Relaxing \( D/t_{\max} \) from \( 40 \) to \( 45 \) would yield an additional \( \sim 0.045\,\mathrm{kg} \) mass saving according to the KKT shadow price \( \partial m^* / \partial (D/t)_{\max} \).
  • Inactive Constraints (\( \mu_i^* = 0 \)): Seat vertical deflection (\( 0.814\,\mathrm{mm} \le 5.0\,\mathrm{mm} \)), first natural frequency (\( 48.50\,\mathrm{Hz} \ge 10.0\,\mathrm{Hz} \)), linear buckling (\( \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} = 3.213 \ge 2.0 \)), and member slenderness (\( 144.98 \le 200.0 \)) remain strictly inside the feasible interior.

7. Mandatory Nonlinear Component (\( N_1 \)): Linearized Buckling Stability

To capture geometric stiffness effects under high compressive sprint forces, we implement Route \( N_1 \) (Linearized Buckling Eigenproblem) in analysis.py and assembly.py. First, the linear static solve \( \mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{F} \) yields member axial forces \( N_e \). Compressive forces (\( N_e < 0 \)) soften lateral bending stiffness via the consistent geometric stiffness matrix \( \mathbf{K}_G(N_e) \), leading to the generalized eigenvalue problem:

$$ \left( \mathbf{K}_{ff} + \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \mathbf{K}_{G,ff}(\mathbf{u}) \right) \boldsymbol{\psi} = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \ge 2.0 $$

Initial Baseline (\( d_0 \)) 1.9156 kg 9.842 118.4 Over-built; large stability reserve
Optimized WITHOUT \( N_1 \) (\( C_6 \) disabled) 1.0597 kg 3.213 144.98 Identical optimum; \( D/t \le 40 \) and \( L/r_g \le 200 \) already prevent Euler buckling
Optimized WITH \( N_1 \) (\( \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \ge 2.0 \)) 1.0597 kg 3.213 144.98 \( N_1 \) active margin +60.7%; buckling constraint verified inactive at optimum
Physical Finding on Nonlinearity (\( N_1 \)): Enabling vs. disabling the linear buckling constraint (\( \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \ge 2.0 \)) produces the exact same optimal mass (\( 1.0597\,\mathrm{kg} \)) because the bottom-bracket stiffness limit (\( \delta_{BB} \le 2.0\,\mathrm{mm} \)) and thin-wall manufacturing bound (\( D/t \le 40 \)) force large outer diameters (\( D_{\mathrm{down}} = 46.57\,\mathrm{mm}, D_{\mathrm{fork}} = 41.77\,\mathrm{mm} \)) with high bending inertia \( I \). Consequently, \( \alpha_{\mathrm{cr}} \) remains safely at \( 3.213 \) (60.7% above the \( 2.0 \) floor). However, if \( D/t_{\max} \) and \( \delta_{BB} \) were relaxed, \( N_1 \) becomes immediately active on the compressed down tube and fork blade during LC2_POS.

9. Engineering Limitations, Reproducibility & AI-Use Statement

9.1 Reproducibility

All verification metrics, optimization histories, and figures on this page are generated deterministically from our repository scripts (model.py, sections.py, frame_element.py, assembly.py, analysis.py, sensitivity.py, problem.py, optimization.py, verification.py) writing directly to results.json.

9.2 Modeling Limitations

  • Provisional Shape Envelope: In the converged results.json run, the 14 cross-sectional variables (\( D_1\dots D_7, t_1\dots t_7 \)) were optimized while the 11 joint coordinates remained fixed at the provisional geometry envelope. Unlocking full 25-variable simultaneous sizing + shape optimization requires multi-start globalization due to non-convexity of member lengths.
  • Joint Compliance & Weld Heat-Affected Zones (HAZ): Frame nodes are modeled as rigid point intersections without gusset fillets or localized 6061-T6 weld HAZ yield knockdown factors (\( S_{y,\mathrm{HAZ}} \approx 150\,\mathrm{MPa} \)), which motivates our conservative \( \sigma_{\mathrm{allow}} = 138\,\mathrm{MPa} \) (\( S_y / 2 \)) limit.
  • SLSQP Feasibility Tolerance: Because SLSQP terminates when constraint violations fall below ftol = 1e-8, active constraints (stress_KS, bottom_bracket_resultant_displacement, maximum_D_over_t) sit at \( +10^{-10} \) to \( +7\times 10^{-9} \), causing the strict boolean flag "feasible": false in results.json even though stress_true_max = 0.99942 < 1.0.

  • What the Coding Agent Wrote: Assisted in scaffolding the modular Python sparse assembly (assembly.py), unit-test harness (verification.py), and Three.js WebGL visualization boilerplate.
  • What the Team Derived & Verified Independently: Our team derived the 25-variable problem formulation, 6061-T6 load cases (LC1, LC2_POS, LC2_NEG), the discrete-adjoint equations, the geometric stiffness buckling formulation (\( N_1 \)), and the analytical reference formulas for V1–V7 prior to running solver checks.
